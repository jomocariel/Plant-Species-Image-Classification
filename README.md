A. Project Overview
The purpose of this study is to develop an image classification model capable of identifying 20 different species of ornamental and tropical plants. We collected over 5,000 plant photographs using Google Teachable Machine and trained a model to discriminate between species based on visual features such as bloom appearance, leaf shape, and color.

B. Plant Species Section 

1.ELEUSINE INDICA

![1](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/be1c3297-cd15-447f-8045-4248f26386f1)

Common Name: Goose Grass / Indian Goosegrass / Yard Grass
Scientific Name: Eleusine indica (L.) Gaertn.
Description: A tufted annual grass growing up to 60 cm tall, native to tropical Asia but now a cosmopolitan weed found in lawns, roadsides, and disturbed soils. It has flat, smooth, linear leaf blades and produces distinctive finger-like seed spikes arranged in a whorl at the stem tip. Traditionally used in folk medicine to treat fever, hypertension, urinary tract infections, and as a diuretic

2.JATROPHA CUSAS

![2](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/193ff70c-6ad6-40fd-b6dd-aaa901561113)

Common Name: Physic Nut / Barbados Nut / Tuba-tuba
Scientific Name: Jatropha curcas L.
Description: A perennial shrub or small tree in the Euphorbiaceae family, growing 3–5 m tall. Native to tropical America, it has large, pale green, deeply lobed leaves and small yellow-green flowers. The seeds contain non-edible oil widely studied for biodiesel production. All parts are toxic if ingested. Commonly used in the Philippines as a living fence and in folk medicine for skin diseases and as a purgative.

3.PLECTRANTHUS SCUTELLARIOIDES

![3](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f2c0ddfa-58d2-435b-977d-f20738c22593)

Common Name: Painted Nettle / Coleus / Mayana
Scientific Name: Plectranthus scutellarioides (L.) R.Br.
Description: A tropical foliage plant in the mint family, native to Southeast Asia and Melanesia. It grows as a bushy annual or perennial up to 1 m tall with broadly ovate, serrated leaves displaying striking combinations of green, purple, red, pink, orange, and yellow. Widely cultivated as an ornamental. In Philippine folk medicine, the leaves are applied as poultices for wounds, burns, bruises, and skin inflammation.

4.RED MAYANA (COLEUS)

![4](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9fe89de8-0ddf-47d4-858c-0a4f5b756d48)

Common Name: Red Mayana / Red Coleus
Scientific Name: Plectranthus scutellarioides (L.) R.Br. (red-leaved cultivar)
Description: A vibrant cultivar of the Coleus group characterized by deep red to crimson foliage, often bordered with green or yellow margins. Widely grown as an ornamental in Philippine gardens and households. In local folk medicine, the red variety is specifically used topically for wounds, bruises, fever, and skin inflammation, and is also taken internally for menstrual irregularities and stomach complaints.

5.CHAMAECOSTUS CUSPIDATUS

![5](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/40663f8d-5dab-4e56-8780-124ceaab88e3)

Common Name: Insulin Plant / Fiery Costus / Step Ladder
Scientific Name: Chamaecostus cuspidatus (Nees & Mart.) C.Specht & D.W.Stev.
Description: A succulent-stemmed perennial herb native to Brazil, now widely cultivated in tropical Asia. It grows 60–90 cm tall with spirally arranged, glossy green leaves with distinctive purple undersides and produces orange-red flowers at the stem tip. The leaves are regularly consumed in folk medicine to manage blood sugar levels, earning it the popular name "insulin plant." Widely recommended for Type 2 diabetes management in alternative medicine.

6.TALINUM PANICULATUM

![6](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/50f4108a-f248-4e46-b6fc-2de8a9b23663)

Common Name: Jewels of Opar / Fame Flower / Waterleaf
Scientific Name: Talinum paniculatum (Jacq.) Gaertn.
Description: A fleshy perennial herb native to tropical America, growing 30–90 cm tall. It has succulent, bright green, oval leaves and produces small, delicate pink to red flowers on branching panicles. The ornamental red-orange seed capsules give it the name "Jewels of Opar." The leaves are consumed as a nutritious vegetable in the Philippines and Southeast Asia, and the plant is used in folk medicine as an aphrodisiac and general health tonic.

7.PSEUDELEPHANTOPUS SPICATUS

![7](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/75d73031-290f-42a8-b02f-3a57b55d1453)

Common Name: Spiked False Elephant's Foot / Dog's Tongue
Scientific Name: Pseudelephantopus spicatus (B.Juss. ex Aubl.) C.F.Baker
Description: A perennial herb in the daisy family, native to tropical America but naturalized throughout tropical Asia and Africa. It grows 30–80 cm tall with erect, branching stems and oblong, rough-textured leaves. Small white flower heads are arranged in elongated spikes along the upper stems. Commonly found in disturbed habitats and roadsides. Used in traditional medicine for treating coughs, respiratory ailments, wounds, kidney problems, and as an anti-inflammatory.

8.CURCUMA LONGA

![8](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/547fd877-c6f2-4526-b2f0-bcbd87833601)

Common Name: Turmeric / Luyang Dilaw (Philippines)
Scientific Name: Curcuma longa L.
Description: A rhizomatous herbaceous perennial native to the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia, growing 1–1.5 m tall with large aromatic leaves and pale yellow flowers. The underground rhizome is vivid orange inside and is the source of the spice turmeric, rich in the bioactive compound curcumin. Widely used in cooking, traditional medicine, cosmetics, and as a natural dye. Scientifically recognized for its powerful anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties.

9.CAPSICUM GENUS

![9](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/fb8fba45-3dc2-4628-b758-0158cf8bebb9)

Common Name: Chili Pepper / Hot Pepper / Sili (Philippines)
Scientific Name: Capsicum spp. (incl. C. annuum, C. frutescens, C. chinense)
Description: A genus of flowering plants in the nightshade family, native to the Americas and now cultivated worldwide. Plants grow as annuals or short-lived perennials with white flowers and fleshy fruits ranging from mild bell peppers to intensely hot varieties. The pungent heat is produced by capsaicin. Extensively used in cooking, food preservation, and medicine. Capsaicin has documented analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and metabolism-boosting properties.

10.IPOMOEA AQUATICA

![10](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/47f098a2-d4ea-4340-af5c-af6d69fca97b)

Common Name: Water Spinach / Kangkong / Swamp Cabbage
Scientific Name: Ipomoea aquatica Forssk.
Description: A semi-aquatic tropical plant native to Southeast Asia and one of the most commonly consumed leafy vegetables in the Philippines. It has hollow, floating stems and arrow-shaped to lanceolate leaves, with attractive white or light purple funnel-shaped flowers. Kangkong thrives in water or moist soil. It is highly nutritious, rich in iron, vitamins A and C, and is also used in folk medicine for its laxative and anti-diabetic properties.

11.CORCHORUS

![11](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9ac76581-6c4c-4530-a76b-64f26c0cdae0)

Common Name: Jute / Saluyot / Nalta Jute
Scientific Name: Corchorus olitorius L. / Corchorus capsularis L.
Description: A genus of approximately 40–100 species in the Malvaceae family, cultivated widely in tropical and subtropical regions. Corchorus species are among the world's most important sources of natural bast fiber (jute). The plant grows 1–3 m tall with small yellow flowers and elongated seed capsules. In the Philippines, the leaves (Saluyot) are cooked as a nutritious vegetable. The leaves are high in vitamins, calcium, and iron and are also used to treat dysentery and fever.

12.EUPHORBIA NERIIFOLIA

![12](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/50257ac6-6cb7-4020-9cd8-9800a9577b0a)

Common Name: Indian Spurge Tree / Holy Milk Hedge / Aludig
Scientific Name: Euphorbia neriifolia L.
Description: A succulent shrub or small tree native to India, growing up to 6 m tall. It has a thick, cylindrical, distinctly segmented and spiny stem with fleshy, dark green leaves that are shed during dry seasons. Like all euphorbias, it produces a milky latex that is toxic and caustic. Used in traditional Ayurvedic and Filipino folk medicine for treating asthma, skin diseases, rheumatism, and intestinal parasites. Commonly planted as a living hedge in tropical gardens.

13.KALANCHOE

![13](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f32be757-a410-48f4-bdd7-b617905758fa)

Common Name: Kalanchoe / Katakataka / Life Plant / Miracle Leaf
Scientific Name: Kalanchoe spp. (incl. K. pinnata, K. blossfeldiana)
Description: A large genus of about 125 succulent plants native to Madagascar and tropical Africa. Kalanchoes have thick, fleshy leaves often with scalloped edges and produce clusters of small, brightly colored flowers. Kalanchoe pinnata (Katakataka) is particularly notable in Philippine folk medicine — the fresh leaves are applied to wounds, burns, boils, and insect bites, and are taken internally for kidney stones, hypertension, and respiratory infections. Also a popular ornamental houseplant.

14.ALTERNANTHERA SESSILIS

![14](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b8d0d583-fa74-424b-86ff-144b7e90d666)

Common Name: Sessile Joyweed / Dwarf Copperleaf / Paco-paco
Scientific Name: Alternanthera sessilis (L.) R.Br. ex DC.
Description: A creeping perennial herb found in moist habitats across tropical and subtropical Asia, Africa, and the Americas. It has prostrate to ascending stems with opposite, narrow, lanceolate leaves that may be green or reddish. Small white flower heads cluster in the leaf axils. The plant is a common weed but also widely consumed as a leafy vegetable and used in traditional medicine for eye ailments, liver conditions, headaches, dysentery, and as a diuretic and coolant.

15.TSAANG GUBAT

![15](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/87ca2ead-49e0-4c54-9281-47896bea226c)

Common Name: Tsaang Gubat / Wild Tea / Forest Tea
Scientific Name: Carmona retusa (Vahl) Masam.
Description: A native Philippine shrub growing 1–5 m tall with small, dark green, rough-textured leaves and tiny white flowers. It is one of the ten herbal medicines officially endorsed by the Philippine Department of Health (DOH). The leaves are brewed as a herbal tea used clinically for stomach aches, diarrhea, flatulence, and colic. Scientific studies have confirmed its antibacterial, antispasmodic, and anti-diarrheal properties, validating its long history in traditional Philippine medicine.

16.SPHAGNETICOLA TRILOBATA

![16](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3ad5e35d-0a61-48b0-a046-a51c76b09994)

Common Name: Wedelia / Bay Biscayne Creeper / Singapore Daisy
Scientific Name: Sphagneticola trilobata (L.) Pruski
Description: A creeping, mat-forming perennial herb native to Central America and the Caribbean, now widely naturalized throughout tropical Asia and considered invasive in many regions. It has succulent, three-lobed, dark green leaves and produces bright yellow daisy-like flowers year-round. Spreads aggressively via stolons. Used as a ground cover but classified as an environmental weed in several countries. Traditionally applied for skin infections, fever, and inflammation.

17.CYANTHILLIUM CINEREUM

![17](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/7179d363-8cbd-4cd8-90b2-c6493ac5f169)

Common Name: Little Ironweed / Ash-colored Fleabane / Pukpukan
Scientific Name: Cyanthillium cinereum (L.) H.Rob.
Description: A common annual or short-lived perennial herb found across tropical Africa, Asia, and Australia. It grows 20–80 cm tall with alternate, ovate, softly hairy leaves and bears small, purple to lilac composite flower heads. Often found along roadsides and in open waste areas. Widely used across Asia in traditional medicine for treating fever, malaria, urinary disorders, skin conditions, cough, and helminthic infections. The plant has documented antipyretic and antimicrobial properties.

18.PORTULACA OLERACEA

![18](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/67144ac4-5934-44d6-93d4-e06218d9c266)

Common Name: Common Purslane / Verdolaga / Alusiman
Scientific Name: Portulaca oleracea L.
Description: A succulent annual herb with a cosmopolitan distribution, considered one of the most widespread plants on Earth. It has reddish, prostrate stems with small, fleshy, spoon-shaped leaves and produces tiny yellow flowers that open only in the morning. Though commonly regarded as a weed, it is one of the most nutritious edible plants known — remarkably rich in omega-3 fatty acids, vitamins A, C, and E, and minerals. Used in folk medicine for digestive ailments, inflammation, fever, and wound healing.

19.STONE BREAKER

![19](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d7f98c57-c8b3-422d-bb9f-d32beadb8367)

Common Name: Stone Breaker / Gale of the Wind / Chanca Piedra / Sampa-sampalukan
Scientific Name: Phyllanthus niruri L.
Description: A tropical annual herb native to the Amazon rainforest, now distributed throughout tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. It grows 20–60 cm tall with numerous small, elliptical leaves arranged in two rows along the branches, creating a feathery appearance, with tiny greenish flowers and fruits beneath the leaves. Globally renowned in herbal medicine for its ability to dissolve kidney and gallbladder stones. Also used for liver disease (hepatitis B), diabetes, hypertension, and viral infections, with growing scientific support.

20.IPOMOEA BATATAS

![20](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c21a383b-b654-4a47-98d1-435d91885b89)

Common Name: Sweet Potato / Kamote / Kamote-cue
Scientific Name: Ipomoea batatas (L.) Lam.
Description: A major root crop native to tropical America, now cultivated worldwide as a food staple and one of the most important crops in the Philippines. It produces long, trailing vines with heart-shaped to lobed leaves and funnel-shaped purple or white flowers. The starchy, sweet tuberous roots range in flesh color from white to deep orange or purple. Both roots and young leaves are highly nutritious and edible. Orange-fleshed varieties are rich in beta-carotene. Also used in folk medicine for managing blood sugar and as an antioxidant source.

1. How did the number of images per class affect your model’s accuracy?
   -The amount of photos per class significantly influenced the model's performance. I discovered that plant species with more photos were predicted more accurately since the model had more data to work with. When a class contained fewer photos, the model became confused and generated more errors. I also discovered that having a balanced number of photos per class allowed the model to perform more fairly. When one class had more photos than the others, the model favored that class during prediction.
2. Which plant species were most commonly misclassified and why?
   -The most typically mistaken species were those that appeared to be quite similar to one another. Many plants had nearly identical leaf color, shape, and feel, therefore the model had difficulties recognizing them. I also noted that lighting and background had an important influence. Some photographs were taken in varied lighting settings or with distracting backdrops, making it difficult for the model to focus on the leaf details.
   
3. How did changing the epochs, batch size, or learning rate affect the training results?
   -When I raised the number of epochs, the model improved initially because it had more time to learn from the data. However, I realized that too many epochs could result in overfitting, in which the model simply memorizes the training images rather than learning general patterns.
I found that smaller batch sizes made training slower but more stable. Larger batch sizes made it faster, but the results were not always consistent.
A reduced learning rate makes the training slower but more precise. If the learning rate was set too high, the training became unstable, and the accuracy shifted.

4. What challenges did you encounter during dataset collection and labeling?
   -One of the most difficult problems was gathering enough photos for each plant type. Some plants were more difficult to find, so the dataset got slightly skewed. Another problem was ensuring that the photographs were clear and correctly captioned. Even a single wrongly tagged image could have an impact on the model's performance. Lighting, shadows, and background distractions also reduced the effectiveness of several photographs as training tools.
   
5. If you were to improve your model, what specific changes would you make and why?
   -If I were to improve the model, I would collect additional photographs for each class to create a more balanced dataset. I'd also try to snap photographs in better lighting and with a plain background to eliminate noise. I would explore more thoroughly with training parameters such as epochs and learning rate to find the ideal combination. Finally, I would include additional diverse photos, such as different angles and sizes of leaves, to help the model generalize more effectively in real-world scenarios.
